Farrier Guide

How much do farriers charge?

Farrier pricing varies widely by region, specialty, and the horse in front of you. These ballpark ranges give a sense of the market — but your local rates and your own costs should set your numbers.

Ballpark ranges

  • Trim only. A straightforward barefoot trim commonly runs in the ballpark of $30 to $50.
  • Full set of shoes. Four new shoes often lands somewhere around $120 to $200+, depending on the region and the work involved.
  • Specialty and therapeutic work. Corrective shoeing, bar shoes, glue-ons, and pads cost more — reflecting the skill, materials, and time.

What moves your price

Region and cost of living, the horse's behavior and hoof condition, travel distance, and your own experience all factor in. Therapeutic and corrective work commands more because it takes more skill and time.

Whatever you charge, know your costs — fuel, materials, insurance, and your time — and price so a full day is actually profitable. Keeping clients on cycle is what turns a fair rate into a steady income.

Frequently asked

Why do farrier prices vary so much?

Rates depend on your region, the type of work (a simple trim vs. corrective shoeing), travel, materials, and experience. Prices in this guide are general ballparks — always check what's typical in your area.

Should farriers charge a travel fee?

Many do, especially for distant or single-horse stops. Grouping clients by area and routing efficiently is another way to protect your margins without raising every price.

How do farriers keep pricing and clients straight?

Keeping notes on each client and horse — including what you did and when — makes pricing and follow-ups consistent. Shoein' stores your clients, horses, and service history in one place.
