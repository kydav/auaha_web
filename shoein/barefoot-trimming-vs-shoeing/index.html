Horseshoeing 101

Barefoot trimming vs shoeing

Not every horse needs shoes. Whether a horse does best barefoot or shod depends on its workload, terrain, hoof quality, and health — and either way, it needs a farrier on a regular cycle.

When barefoot works

Many horses live happily barefoot, especially those with strong, healthy hooves in light-to-moderate work on forgiving ground. Going barefoot lets the hoof flex and wear naturally, and it's simpler and cheaper — but it still requires regular, skilled trims to stay balanced.

A good barefoot trim is not 'no maintenance' — it's the same cycle, just without the shoe.

When shoes earn their keep

  • Protection. Hard, rocky, or abrasive terrain and heavy work can wear a hoof faster than it grows.
  • Traction. Certain disciplines and conditions call for the grip shoes provide.
  • Therapeutic support. Corrective and therapeutic shoeing can support horses with specific hoof or limb issues, on a vet's and farrier's guidance.

Frequently asked

Is it better for a horse to be barefoot or shod?

It depends on the individual horse — its work, terrain, hoof quality, and any health needs. Many horses do well barefoot; others need shoes for protection, traction, or therapeutic support. Your farrier (and vet, when relevant) can advise for a specific horse.

Do barefoot horses need a farrier?

Yes — barefoot horses need regular trims on roughly the same 6-to-8-week cycle to keep the hoof balanced and healthy.

Can a horse transition from shod to barefoot?

Often, yes, with a planned transition and good trimming — but it should be done thoughtfully with your farrier, since it depends on the horse's feet and workload.
