The essential farrier tool kit

A farrier's shop travels in the truck. Here are the core tools behind a trim and a shoeing, and what each one is for.

The trimming and shoeing kit

  • Hoof nippers. Trim excess hoof wall to length.
  • Hoof knife. Clean and pare the sole, frog, and bars.
  • Rasp. Level and finish the trim, and smooth the wall.
  • Pull-offs. Remove old shoes.
  • Driving hammer & clinchers. Drive and set the nails, then clinch them to hold the shoe.
  • Hoof stand. Support the foot while you finish and clinch.

For shaping and hot work

An anvil and shaping hammer let a farrier adjust shoes to the foot. A forge is added for hot shoeing — heating shoes to shape and fit them precisely.

Round it out with protective gear like an apron or chaps, plus a supply of shoes and nails in the sizes you use most.

Frequently asked

What tools does a farrier need to start?

At minimum: nippers, a hoof knife, a rasp, pull-offs, a driving hammer, clinchers, and a hoof stand, plus shoes and nails. An anvil and forge come in for shaping and hot shoeing.

Do farriers need a forge?

Only for hot shoeing. Many farriers cold shoe with an anvil and hammer, and add a forge when they want to shape or fit shoes hot.
