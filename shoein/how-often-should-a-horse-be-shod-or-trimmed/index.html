How often should a horse be shod or trimmed?

As a rule of thumb, most horses need a farrier every six to eight weeks — but hoof growth, the season, and the horse's workload all move that number. Here's how to think about the cycle and stay ahead of it.

The typical cycle: 6 to 8 weeks

A hoof grows roughly a quarter to three-eighths of an inch per month, so left unattended it steadily gets long, out of balance, and prone to chipping or flare. Trimming and resetting on a regular cycle keeps the hoof balanced and the horse comfortable.

Six to eight weeks is the standard window for both shod and barefoot horses. Shod horses need resets before the shoe shifts or the hoof outgrows it; barefoot horses need trims to keep the wall and sole in balance.

What moves the timing

  • Season. Hooves grow faster in warm months and slower in winter, so a horse on an 8-week winter cycle may need 5 to 6 weeks in summer.
  • Workload and discipline. Performance horses, and any horse on hard or abrasive ground, often need more frequent attention.
  • Hoof quality and conformation. Cracks, flares, thin walls, or corrective work can shorten the cycle.
  • Barefoot vs shod. Barefoot horses can sometimes stretch the cycle slightly if they're wearing evenly, but they still need regular trims.

Frequently asked

What happens if you wait too long between farrier visits?

Overgrown hooves throw the foot out of balance, which stresses tendons and joints and raises the risk of cracks, flares, and lost shoes. Staying on a regular cycle is far easier on the horse than catching up after a long gap.

Do barefoot horses still need a farrier?

Yes. Barefoot horses need regular trims — usually on the same 6-to-8-week rhythm — to keep the hoof balanced, even if they don't wear shoes.
