Can I Show This House?

A 30-second buyer-agreement compliance check. Answer a few questions and find out whether you're clear to show — or need a signature first.

How the rule works

Since August 17, 2024, real estate agents who work through a Multiple Listing Service must have a written buyer agreement in place before touring a home with a buyer — in person or on a live virtual tour. It's part of the nationwide changes that followed the NAR settlement.

You generally need a signed agreement when…

  • You're touring a specific home together — the trigger is showing property, in person or virtually.
  • You're representing the buyer as an MLS participant or on an MLS-listed home.

You generally don't need one just to…

  • Host an open house, or let a buyer attend one you're hosting.
  • Talk, email, or send listings before you tour a home together.

What the agreement must include

  • A specific, objective compensation amount agreed with the buyer — not open-ended.
  • Scope and time period of your representation.
  • The buyer's signature, dated before the first showing.

Get it signed in the field
